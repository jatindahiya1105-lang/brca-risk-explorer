Key findings

  • Expression and methylation independently predictive — both achieve AUC well above chance on their own, despite being completely different molecular data types.
  • Naively combining all four layers underperforms expression alone (0.747 vs. 0.776) — with only ~325 training patients and 11,000+ combined features, the two weak layers (mutation, CNV) add noise rather than signal. A defensible, reportable finding about dimensionality vs. sample size, not a pipeline failure.
  • Mutation and CNV carry little independent signal for this risk stratification (AUC near 0.55) — despite TP53 and PIK3CA (the two most common BRCA driver mutations) topping the mutation SHAP ranking, suggesting this risk signature reflects transcriptional/epigenetic state more than driver-mutation identity.
  • The methylation-driven gene set does not overlap with the expression-based proliferation/EMT signature (0/30 direct gene overlap), and shows no significant pathway enrichment of its own — an honest null result on whether the same signature is visible at the epigenetic level.

Convergent validation. HMMR, RRM2, and DLGAP5 form the network's most-connected cluster — the same proliferation/cell-cycle gene module (Hallmark "E2F Targets") independently flagged by pathway enrichment analysis. A separate LAMB3–LAMC2 pair reflects the laminin-332 complex, consistent with the ECM/EMT theme from the same enrichment results. Three independent methods — SHAP + literature review, pathway over-representation analysis, and PPI network topology — converge on the same two biological themes via completely different data sources. Note: STRING's "multiple proteins" mode only draws edges among genes in this specific list, so an isolated node reflects no shared partner within this list, not an absence of real biological interactions elsewhere.
